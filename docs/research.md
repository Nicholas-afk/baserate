## Existing tools and the originality boundary

[Seeing Theory's Bayesian Inference chapter](https://seeing-theory.brown.edu/bayesian-inference/index.html) already teaches a rare-condition medical-test example with adjustable probabilities, patient simulation and posterior counts. [Tool Corner's Bayes Medical Test Visualiser](https://toolcorner.net/labs/bayes-medical-test-visualiser) documents sliders/numeric fields, population icons, four outcomes, PPV/NPV and presets. These are primary creator pages. This comparison inspects their published features; it is not a controlled usability comparison or proof that no other equivalent product exists.

BaseRate's probability calculator, icon population and natural-frequency explanation are established approaches. Prediction and scaffolding are also established teaching methods. Its plausible contribution is connecting first predictions, denominator correction, a retained notebook, editable comparisons, dependence assumptions and inspectable local AI topic suggestions in one small, reproducible static lab. This integration may be useful; novelty and effectiveness remain to be judged.

## Evidence boundaries and next validation

Automated arithmetic tests establish behavior for specified inputs. Browser inspection establishes that the inspected controls/states function in the inspected environment. Synthetic examples and an automated code review establish development findings, not learner reactions. None measures comprehension, retention, confidence calibration, accessibility with assistive-technology users or health outcomes.

The next meaningful study would use independently collected explanations and unfamiliar transfer questions, preserve first answers, compare a suitable control condition, and measure delayed comprehension as well as immediate correction. Selecting the correct option after feedback is practice completion; it is not demonstrated learning gain. No participant study has been conducted.
